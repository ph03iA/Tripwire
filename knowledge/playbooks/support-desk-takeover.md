# Playbook: takeover through the support desk

## How it looks
- A caller asks the support desk for a password reset and passes the security questions (KBA). The answers are often found online or in leaked data.
- The call comes from a phone that isn't on the account, often from another city.
- Minutes later the password is reset through the support channel, a new device signs in, and a large transfer goes to a new payee.

## Signals that matter
- A reset or email-change request verified only by security questions, or not verified at all.
- A caller phone that isn't the registered one, especially from outside the home city.
- A password reset made from a new device, and a login soon after a support-desk reset.
- A transfer of ₹50,000 or more to a new payee.

## Response
- Step up the reset itself with a code to the registered phone. A caller who only knows the answers can't pass.
- Never change the registered phone on a call verified only by security questions.
- Review any login within an hour of a support-desk reset.

## Look-alikes
Real customers do call in for resets and pass the questions, sometimes from a work phone. They then sign in on their own phone and don't move large sums to new payees. A step-up costs them one code.
